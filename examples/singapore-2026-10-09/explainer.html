Finding ships from orbit with radar

A satellite bounces microwaves off the sea. Calm water sends almost nothing back; a steel hull sends back a bright spot. This pipeline downloads that radar image, runs a pretrained ship detector over it, and turns every bright hull into a point on a map.

The pipeline

Five steps, one command

  1. 1

    Download

    Ask Copernicus for the newest Sentinel-1 pass over the box, on the same 9.55 m Web Mercator grid the model was trained on.

    download_s1.py
  2. 2

    Convert

    Turn calibrated backscatter back into the raw brightness the model expects: DN = 237 × √β⁰, capped at 255.

    VH + VV, 8-bit
  3. 3

    Detect

    Slide the Allen Institute for AI's Faster R-CNN over 2,048 px windows. Every bright hull gets a point and a confidence score.

    1,373 candidates ≥ 0.2
  4. 4

    Clean up

    Keep scores ≥ 0.5, merge hits within 190 m, and drop anything on land or the shoreline.

    842 → 764
  5. 5

    Describe

    A second Ai2 model reads a 120 px crop around each ship and estimates its length, width, heading and speed.

    CSV + GeoJSON

What it found

764 vessels, mostly at anchor

Singapore's anchorages hold hundreds of ships waiting for berths or bunkering, and that is what the radar shows: dense grids of stationary hulls off the south coast and in the eastern approaches.

Estimated length

The smallest ships the model reliably sees are about 40 m long, roughly four pixels.

How far to trust it

Checked by eye, not yet against ship tracking data

Solid

Finding ships

Of 665 very bright spots on the water, 591 have a detection within 140 m. Most of the rest are glare streaks from ships already found, or piers.

Solid

Length and speed

40–330 m with a median of 88 m, and 85% of ships below 1 knot, which fits anchorages.

Rough

Heading and fishing

Headings bunch around 67–90°, and 114 "likely fishing" vessels is too many for these waters.

Missing

Docked ships, accuracy numbers

The land mask drops ships at piers. Real precision and recall need matching against AIS ship positions.
